Sources & methodology

How the traffic light works

Zoomed out (regional view): each bubble is a cluster of stations. Its colour compares the area's average price with the national average of each station's own country — green ≤ −1.5%, red ≥ +1.5%, amber in between. This keeps colours meaningful across borders.

Zoomed in (station view): colours re-rank against the stations currently on screen — cheapest third green, middle third amber, priciest third red — so you can always spot the best stop on a route.

How to read each price — trust tiers

  • 🟢 Government · per station — official national price feeds, refreshed every run. Highest trust; use these to decide where to fuel.
  • 🔵 Official national average — one figure per country (EU Weekly Oil Bulletin), drawn as a dashed bubble at the country centre. Official but not per-station — indicative for planning only.
  • 🟡 Community — Open Charge Map (EV). Charger location & power are reliable; price is community-supplied and often missing.
  • ⚪ Location only — grey dot, no price (OpenStreetMap), for diesel in countries with no public price feed. Shows where to fuel, not the price.

Methodology & updates

Everything refreshes automatically every day at 06:00 UTC (plus any manual run). If an official feed is down on refresh day (Spain’s server, for example, rejects connections some days), the last good data is kept and marked, and the next run replaces it — the map never fabricates a price. UK prices convert £→€ at the daily ECB rate. EV prices are sanity-checked (values above €1.20/kWh are treated as non-euro or per-session tariffs and dropped). OpenStreetMap locations are cached in the repository, so a country’s stations never disappear if OSM rate-limits a run. National averages marked ≈ are the official EU weekly bulletin figures (or pinned indicative values for Norway and Switzerland), not station-level prices. EU Weekly Oil Bulletin in use: — (diesel, prices with taxes).

Known gaps & honest limits

So planners trust the numbers, here is what the map does not have: Per-station prices exist for 6 countries only (Germany, Spain, France, Italy, UK, Austria) — the other 15 show the EU national average, not individual station prices. Germany lists about 14,300 of ~15,000 stations; the rest are card-only or depot pumps that do not report to the public feed. HVO100 per-station prices exist only in Spain and Italy (nobody else publishes them). EV shows a price for roughly 18% of chargers — the rest are plan- or roaming-based and have no public price (location and kW are still reliable). Prices can lag between refreshes; Germany updates when its local sweep is re-run.

Route planning (truck / HGV)

💶 Saving per fill is the price spread along the route (dearest minus cheapest priced station within your chosen km) multiplied by the average litres per fill for that vehicle class. Those averages come from ATC's own DKV and AS24 fuel card transactions, January to May 2026: Diesel (incl. Special & Premium) 321.6 L/fill for trucks (Rigid + Tractor + Truck, 360 fills) and 52.7 L/fill for vans (757 fills); HVO100 (incl. Biodiesel) 277.7 L/fill for trucks (744 fills) and 60.0 L/fill for vans (9 fills — a small sample, so treat the van HVO figure as indicative). It is a best-case figure: it assumes the whole fill is taken at the cheapest station rather than the dearest, and it does not yet subtract the cost of detouring to reach that station. EV per-charge saving is not calculated yet, as average kWh per charging session has not been loaded.

🛣 Routes come from OpenRouteService (openrouteservice.org) using its heavy-goods-vehicle profile on OpenStreetMap road data — real routing that avoids general truck-restricted roads (it is not yet tuned to a specific vehicle’s weight/height). Prices shown along a route are the same official station data as the map, and any country the route crosses that does not publish per-station prices is flagged as national-average only. “km off route” is measured to the nearest sampled point on the route line (accurate to within a few km), and the country flag is inferred from the nearest station, so it may be approximate right at a border.

EV charging — all countries (community data)

Where it comes from: ⚡ Open Charge Map (openchargemap.org), the largest open registry of charge points, covering all 21 countries in this tool. For every charger it gives location, power rating (kW), operator, and connector type (AC / DC / HPC).

Limitations: Most chargers have no public price because EV tariffs depend on the driver’s charging plan or roaming contract. No official live EV-price feed exists anywhere in Europe, so per-kWh price cannot be guaranteed and is shown only where an operator publishes it. Open Charge Map is community-maintained, so a few entries may be out of date. Location and power are reliable — that is what the Van/Truck/HPC power filter at the top uses.

Refresh: pulled fresh every day at 06:00 UTC together with the rest of the data, plus any time the pipeline is run manually.

Updates

Refreshes automatically every day at 06:00 UTC via an automated pipeline; you can also trigger it manually. No sample or estimated station data is ever shown — if a country or fuel has no trusted source, it stays empty.
